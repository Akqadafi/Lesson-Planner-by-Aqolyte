Lesson Plan

Grade Level: 10

Number of Students: 12

State: Alabama


- Computers, iPads or Chromebooks with internet access - Digital mapping software or applications (Google Earth) - Collaboration and presentation tools (Prezi, Google Slides, Padlet)

1. Begin the lesson by asking students what they know about Germany. Prompt them to mention basic geography, climate, and prominent cities. This will get students thinking about the topic.

1. Identify Three major physical features in Germany and explain their geographic importance.

1. Identify key geographic features of Germany. 2. Understand the diverse climates and landscapes of Germany. 3. Analyze how the geography influences the culture, natural resources, and economy of Germany. 4. Work collaboratively to gather, interpret and present geographical data.

1. Once research is complete, have students synthesize their information and prepare a brief presentation.

1. Provide students with a list of credible online sources for their research. Encourage them to make use of digital mapping tools like Google Earth to visualize the locations they are researching.

1. Summarize key points from each group's presentation, reinforcing the diversity and impact of Germany's geography.

2. As the students conduct their research, encourage them to note interesting facts, extract key data, and save relevant images or graphics.

2. Ask students to reflect on how technology facilitated their exploration and understanding of Germany.

2. Compare two major cities in Germany, discuss their geographic location, climate, and notable physical features.

2. Each group will present their findings to the class. Open the floor for discussion after each presentation allowing students to ask questions or discuss interesting insights.

2. Introduce the WebQuest and its objectives. Group students in pairs or threes and assign each group a specific aspect of Germany's geography - physical features, climate diversity, key cities and their locations, and how geography influences culture and economy.

3. How does the geography of Germany affect its culture, economy or use of natural resources? Provide specific examples from your research.

3. Remind students to document their findings on their digital worksheets provided.

4. Using Google Earth, locate and capture an image of one geographic feature or city you discussed in your presentation. Explain why you chose this.

Learning Objectives and Outcomes

Lesson Overview This lesson makes use of a WebQuest that takes students on a virtual exploration of Germany’s geography. Students will collaborate in small groups to research different aspects of German geography, crafting a vivid portrait of the country's diverse landscape.

Lesson Procedure

Materials Needed

Research and Exploration (45 minutes)

SAMR Connection

Substitution Students research online instead of using textbooks. Augmentation Students use Google Earth for a different perspective. Modification Students collaborate in small groups on a shared document instead of individual work. Redefinition Student knowledge is broadened through the WebQuest, transforming traditional geography classes into an interactive, immersive experience.

This WebQuest allows the teacher to monitor and guide the students' learning without controlling it, encouraging exploration, and authentic inquiry in a fun, engaging way. It also introduces students to the SAMR model and encourages the use of technology in facilitating learning.
